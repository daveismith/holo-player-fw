Host link: CAN¶

On CAN, the board is a node on a Cyphal bus: Cyphal/CAN, on classic CAN frames. It suits a build that already has a CAN bus through the body and dome, with other Cyphal nodes on it. The board on Cyphal covers what the node does once it's wired: how it gets its node-ID, what it reports, and its settings.

Over Cyphal a controller can do most of what a host does over UART or RS485: move the holo and set its light, apply scenes, show clips and stills, set the LED ring, hear the board's events, list the scenes and the files, and calibrate the holo, as well as manage the node with Cyphal's standard registers and commands (the holo on Cyphal). Uploading files, Wi-Fi and the configuration backup stay on Wi-Fi and the USB console.

What you need¶

  • A CAN transceiver with 3.3 V logic. The ESP32-S3 has the CAN controller, and the transceiver puts it on the wires.

    • A TJA1051T/3 is what this page was written with. It runs from 5 V, and the /3 version has a separate pin (VIO) that sets its logic level to 3.3 V.
    • A 3.3 V part, such as an SN65HVD230 or a TCAN332, needs no 5 V at all.

!!! warning "No 5 V on GPIO33" A 5 V transceiver without a VIO pin (a TJA1050, an MCP2551, or a plain TJA1051T) drives its receive output to 5 V. Use one with VIO, or a 3.3 V part.

  • Twisted pair for CANH and CANL, plus a ground wire.
  • Two 120 Ω resistors, one at each end of the bus.
  • Something to talk to. For a first test, a USB CAN adapter on a computer: one that runs the candleLight (gs_usb) firmware, such as a CANable, works with cyphal_service on Linux and macOS.

Wiring a TJA1051T/3¶

Transceiver Pin Goes to P2 pin
TXD 1 GPIO21 11
GND 2 GND 1 or 5
VCC 3 5 V —
RXD 4 GPIO33 12
VIO 5 3V3 6
CANL 6 the bus —
CANH 7 the bus —
S 8 GND 1 or 5
  • VIO to 3V3. It sets the level of TXD and RXD. Left unconnected, the board receives nothing.
  • S to GND. S high is silent mode: the board hears the bus but never sends, and never acknowledges anyone else's frames; its controller goes bus-off as soon as it tries. Left unconnected, it may float high.
  • 5 V for VCC. The part wants 4.5–5.5 V. P2's VSYS pin is not a 5 V rail: it is about 4.6 V on USB, which is just inside the range, and the cell's voltage on a battery, which is not. Take 5 V from the bus's supply or a regulator.
  • A 100 nF capacitor from VCC to GND, and another from VIO to GND, close to the part.

A 3.3 V transceiver is the same with fewer wires: its supply to 3V3, TXD (often labelled D or CTX) to GPIO21, RXD (R or CRX) to GPIO33, and its mode pin (RS or S) to GND.

GPIO15, the host link's third pin, isn't used on CAN.

The bus¶

   adapter                    board                  another node
  120 Ω H–L                                           120 Ω H–L
  CANH ─────────────────────── CANH ─────────────────────── CANH
  CANL ─────────────────────── CANL ─────────────────────── CANL
   GND ──────────────────────── GND ──────────────────────── GND
  • One line, not a star, with a short stub to each node.
  • 120 Ω across CANH and CANL at each end, and nowhere else. With the power off, a meter across CANH and CANL reads about 60 Ω when both are there, 120 Ω when one is missing, and 40 Ω when there's one too many. Many adapters and modules have a resistor behind a jumper.
  • Even two nodes on a short cable need both resistors. At 1 Mbit/s an unterminated bus doesn't work.
  • Connect the grounds.
  • Every node uses the same bitrate. The board's is 1 Mbit/s unless changed. It samples each bit at 85 % of its length, at every bitrate it offers (20 time quanta; SJW 1): the nearest the ESP32-S3's CAN controller comes to the 87.5 % that CANopen and Cyphal suggest. Nodes a few percent apart work together on a short bus.

Turning it on¶

On the board's USB console:

link mode can
restart

After the restart the node asks the bus for a node-ID by plug-and-play, and cyphal shows it waiting until an allocator answers:

cyphal: waiting for a node-ID, r2.dome.holo.front, 1 Mbit/s on GPIO21 (TX), GPIO33 (RX)

then running on the node-ID it was given, within a second or two of the start:

cyphal: node 2 (allocated), r2.dome.holo.front, 1 Mbit/s on GPIO21 (TX), GPIO33 (RX)

On a bus with no allocator, give it a fixed node-ID instead, any free one from 0 to 125:

cyphal id 52
cyphal restart

It listens for 2 seconds, then runs:

cyphal: node 52 (fixed), r2.dome.holo.front, 1 Mbit/s on GPIO21 (TX), GPIO33 (RX)

A first test¶

With a candleLight adapter on a computer, and the board the only other node, give cyphal_service a transport at the board's bitrate. Its allocator is on unless turned off; its database goes to /var/lib/cyphal_service/ unless pnp_db_path says otherwise, which on macOS it has to, since that folder can't be created there without root:

[[transport]]
type        = "can"
name        = "can-usb"
driver      = "gsusb"
node_id     = 43
bitrate     = 1000000
pnp_db_path = "can-usb-allocations.db"

cyphal_service --foreground --config cyphal.toml

Start the board first, so that the service's heartbeats are acknowledged from the moment it opens the adapter. The board asks for a node-ID, the service gives it the lowest one free in its database, and lists it within a second or two:

cyphal> nodes
Transport: can-usb
  Node ID  Uptime(s)  Health     Mode
  2        4          0          0
  43       15         0          0
cyphal> get-info can-usb 2
Node 2  (r2.dome.holo.front)
Hardware : 1.0
Software : 0.0
Unique ID: 68:6f:6c:6f:70:6c:61:79:00:00:e4:b0:63:8c:2d:b0
cyphal> pnp-list
Transport: can-usb
  Node ID  Unique ID (hex)                    Pseudo UID           Timestamp
  2        686f6c6f706c61790000e4b0638c2db0   156728846609014      2026-11-20 09:27:15
  43       7fda830fd867643c1f2cfe147645ebf8   NULL                 2026-04-04 23:43:52

The service records the board's pseudo-ID when it allocates the node-ID, then asks the board for GetInfo and fills in its unique ID. It gives the same node-ID to the same pseudo-ID from then on.

The service also records every node it sees with a fixed node-ID, by its unique ID alone. A board it first saw that way keeps that row, and plug-and-play gives it a new one, with another node-ID: the service matches a plug-and-play request by its pseudo-ID only. To keep the old node-ID, give the board that one as a fixed node-ID, or remove the old row from the service's database.

If the board doesn't appear, cyphal on its console says what the bus looks like from its side:

  • bus-off, and bus-off … times going up while frames: sent doesn't move: the board can't see its own frames on the bus. A CAN controller reads back every bit it sends; when the bus doesn't follow it, each try at a frame ends in an error, and it takes itself off the bus within the first frame. Either the bus doesn't follow TXD (S isn't at GND, so the transceiver is silent, or the TXD wire is loose) or RXD doesn't follow the bus (the RXD wire, or VIO, which sets RXD's level). Check S, VIO, RXD and TXD. The node keeps recovering, less and less often.
  • tx errors at 128 and the state error-passive: the board's frames get onto the bus but nobody acknowledges them. The adapter isn't open, the bitrates differ, CANH and CANL are swapped, or a termination resistor is missing.
  • rx errors climbing: frames arrive damaged. Usually the bitrate, then the termination.
  • frames: sent going up, received 0: something on the bus acknowledges the board's frames but sends nothing: an adapter that is open with no program sending, say. Check that cyphal_service, or the other node, is running.
  • stopped: node-ID … is in use by another node: see a node-ID that is already taken. cyphal_service is node 43 in the configuration above, so a board given 43 stops this way.

The can command tests the wiring below Cyphal: raw frames in and out, and a self-test that needs no second node.
