Cyphal interface¶

This is the specification of what the board does on a Cyphal/CAN bus: every port, every type and its fields, every register and every command. How to use it is on the board on Cyphal.

Node management uses Cyphal's standard types, from public_regulated_data_types. The holo's own types are r2.holo.*, in a repository of their own, r2_cyphal_dsdl, which a controller (the dome panel, an app on Linux) generates its code from too. They are at version 0.1, unstable: they become 1.0 when the dome panel is first written against them, and from then on a type that has shipped is never changed, only given a new version.

The standard ports' IDs are fixed by the standard. The r2.holo ports' are defaults, in Cyphal's unregulated ranges, which registers change. r2_cyphal_dsdl's ports/holo.csv holds the same defaults for a program to read.

Port name Kind Default ID Priority
status Published subject 2300 low
event Published subject 2301 nominal
axes Published subject 2302 high
setpoint Subscribed subject 2303 fast
motion Served service 100 nominal
light Served service 101 nominal
scene Served service 102 nominal
screen Served service 103 nominal
ring Served service 104 nominal
list_scenes Served service 105 nominal
media_info Served service 106 nominal

The priority is the one the board sends at; a client calls a service at the same. Services are addressed to the board's node-ID, so two holos on one bus serve them on the same IDs without trouble. A subject is shared by the whole bus: two holos can publish status on 2300 (a receiver tells them apart by node-ID), but two holos subscribed to setpoint on 2303 move together, so each gets a subject of its own.

The port list¶

uavcan.node.port.List names the ports the node really has while it runs, at the optional priority, as the type asks. A port's number changes only when the node starts (the registers apply from then), so the list does too: it goes out as the node goes online, and every 10 seconds after. With the registers at their defaults it holds:
Publishers 7509 (the heartbeat), 7510 (the port list), 2300 (status), 2301 (event), 2302 (axes)
Subscribers 2303 (setpoint)
Clients None
Servers 100 (motion), 101 (light), 102 (scene), 103 (screen), 104 (ring), 105 (list_scenes), 106 (media_info), 384 and 385 (registers), 405 and 406 (the file services), 430 (GetInfo), 435 (ExecuteCommand)

Each r2.holo port is in it on the number its register held as the node started; a port turned off (65535) is never in it. The servers are a bit mask of every service-ID, so the list is 160 bytes, 24 frames, whatever it holds.

Health¶

The heartbeat's health is advisory while the holo can't move, for the reasons Status gives in ready and why, and nominal otherwise. The node's mode is always operational. On this board the holo can't move only when one of its servos didn't attach at the start (not fitted), or the holo engine didn't start (Status then reads not fitted for no axis in particular): an axis without saved endpoints moves over its default travel, so it doesn't make the health advisory (Status's calibrated still says whether its endpoints are saved). A board with no holo wired to it at all can't tell: its servos are outputs that read nothing back, so it reports the holo ready, and nominal.

Conventions¶

  • Units are in the names: _ms is milliseconds, _us microseconds. A percent is 0–100 unless the field says otherwise.
  • Zero means the default. A request field left at 0 takes its default wherever it has one, so a request that is all zeros asks for every default. Where 0 is a value in its own right (a move's duration, the light's level, the backlight, the ring's off), the field is optional instead: an array of at most one ([<=1]), empty for the default.
  • Times in Status are spans from the moment it was sent, *_age_ms back and *_remaining_ms forward, never stamps, so the receiver's clock needn't be the board's. NEVER (0xFFFFFFFF) is "no such time".
  • Paths are from the root of the storage volume (/clips/intro.mov), as uavcan.file.Path.2.0: up to 255 bytes in the type, at most 159 on this board.
  • Names of scenes are 1–32 of A-Z a-z 0-9, space, _, . and -.
  • Sizes. Classic CAN carries 7 bytes of a transfer a frame. A transfer of up to 7 bytes is one frame; a longer one adds a 2-byte CRC. Sizes and bus load has each type's.

Messages¶

Status¶

r2.holo.Status.0.1, published on status: everything the holo is doing, in one message, so that each is a consistent snapshot. It goes out within 250 ms of a change, never more than four times a second, and once a second whatever changes. A change is a change to any field but a time or a clip's progress (*_age_ms, *_remaining_ms, shown, passes), which the once-a-second message keeps current. The board builds it from the same state GET /api/v1/holo, GET /api/v1/leds, GET /api/v1/screen and GET /api/v1/scenes (its active) report. Once another Status may go (250 ms after the last), it looks at the holo, the screen and the scene for a change every 50 ms; the ring tells it of its own changes, as it tells the HTTP API's event stream. While the screen is being changed (a still decoding, a clip starting, the panel going to sleep), the board doesn't wait for it: screen stays as it was in the Status that goes meanwhile, and the change follows within 50 ms of its end. With nothing showing and no scene it is 86 bytes, 13 frames; with a 20-byte path and a 10-byte scene name, 116 bytes, 17 frames.

Field Type What it is
holo state.Holo The holo's motion, below
ring state.Ring The LED ring, which is also the holo's light, below
screen state.Screen The screen, below
scene state.Scene The scene running to its end, below
event_seq uint16 The latest Event's seq; 0 before the first. A receiver that sees it move past the last event it got has missed one
reporting_touch bool Touch events are on (the register r2.touch.reporting, or touch on)

Event¶

r2.holo.Event.0.1, published on event: one of the board's happenings, the same ones its JSON events and the host link's event records carry. Each is published once, as it happens, and there is no catching up: a receiver that misses one sees the gap in seq (or in Status's event_seq) and reads Status for what is going on now.

The board publishes them from its own record of happenings, the one the HTTP API's GET /api/v1/events?after= reads, which keeps the last 32: each is sent in turn, oldest first, with the number the record gave it, whatever started it (Cyphal, the console, the HTTP API, the host link or a scene). Happenings that come together (a scene applied over another can make four at once) wait in a queue of 8 for the bus, never replacing one another; if that queue is full, the one it refused is offered again a moment later, then at longer intervals while it stays full, and the rest are read from the record once it has gone. So none is lost while the record still has it, and one it no longer has shows as a jump in seq, as a missed one does. What happened before the node went online isn't sent: the first seq after it may be any number. Status's event_seq is the latest Event's, once it has gone into that queue.

Field Type What it is
seq uint16 The board's number for this happening: 1–65535, then 1 again. One count for every happening on the board, shared with the web app and the host link
what union One of the five below
what Fields The JSON event
clip_started plays uint16 (times through in all, 1–1000; 0 forever), frames uint16 (in one pass), fps float16, path clip_started
clip_ended finished bool (played to its end; false when something replaced or stopped it), path clip_ended
scene_started then uint8 (0 stay, 1 restore, 2 off), until_clip_ends bool, duration_ms uint32 (0 for none), slot uint8 (0 for none), name scene_started
scene_ended then uint8, by uint8 (0 its clip played, 1 its time was up, 2 it was ended, 3 replaced), slot uint8, name scene_ended
screen_touch action uint8 (0 down, 1 up), x, y uint8 (0–239, from the top left). Only while touch reporting is on touch

The paths and names are carried whole, as the JSON events carry them, so a receiver needn't ask which clip ended. The order is the JSON events' order: a scene's clip starts just before its scene_started, and its scene_ended comes before what its then does.

Axes¶

r2.holo.Axes.0.1: both axes, in hundredths of a percent of the calibrated travel each side of the centre. One frame.

Field Type What it is
x int16 Horizontal: -10000 (closed, full left) to 10000 (open, full right); 0 the middle
y int16 Vertical: -10000 (full down) to 10000 (full up)

NONE, -32768, is "no value" in either.

  • Published on axes: where the board last put each axis. A servo reports nothing back, so this is the position commanded, not one measured. NONE for an axis nothing has moved since the board started.
  • Subscribed on setpoint: a direct move. Each setpoint goes to the axes at once, with no easing, and stops the motion running first; the latest wins. NONE leaves that axis where it is. A value beyond ±10000 is held to it. While the holo can't move, a setpoint moves nothing and nothing answers it: Status says why.

The board does what the dome panel does for a ROS joint command: the motion running, if any, is stopped (Status then shows stop as the last motion asked for), and each axis given goes straight to its place through the servo layer, the call the holo engine's own moves end in. A setpoint received while the board is still carrying out the one before replaces it. The first setpoint after the board starts, before anything has placed the axes, goes through the engine as a move with no duration instead, so that the engine knows from then on where they are: an axis given as NONE in that one goes to the centre, where the engine takes an axis nothing has placed to be. A move is in whole percent, so once the engine has made it (within 20 ms) the same setpoint goes again the direct way, to its exact place; Status shows that move as the last motion asked for.

The resolution is finer than the holo's own, so nothing is lost either way: the engine places an axis in steps of 0.2 % of the travel each side (20 hundredths of a percent), and the pulse goes out in whole microseconds, which over this board's default travel is coarser still (0.27 % on pan, 0.31 % on tilt). A position published is the pulse sent, read back against the endpoints, so it can be up to half a percent short of the setpoint that put the axis there.

Services¶

Every service answers with an Error as result: code 0 when it was done. A service does what the same request over the HTTP API or the host link does, through the same code, with the same refusals. A request that opens a file or waits (showing a file, applying a scene) is answered when it is done, a few hundred milliseconds at most; while the board is still working through the requests already sent to it, another is answered busy.

Scene, Screen and MediaInfo requests, the file services (and identify) are carried out one at a time, in the order they came, by a task of their own, so that a still being decoded, a file's header being read or the storage volume being busy with an upload never holds up the setpoints, Status or the other services. Two at most wait there; a third is answered busy (or, for uavcan.file.List, which can't say so, not at all). Motion, Light, Ring and ListScenes requests go on meanwhile, so one sent while a Scene or Screen request is still being carried out may be done before it: a controller that needs them in order waits for each response.

Motion¶

r2.holo.Motion.0.1, on motion: start a motion of the holo, replacing the one running, as POST /api/v1/holo/motion and the console's holo do. The request is one field, motion, an r2.holo.MotionSpec.0.1; three frames.

Field Type What it is
kind uint4 0 center, 1 move, 2 nudge, 3 twitch, 4 wag, 5 nod, 6 scan, 7 circle, 8 stop, 9 off. off stops, lets both axes go limp, and turns the light off: the release. On this board the light is the LED ring, which off turns off (the HTTP API's and the host link's off leave the ring alone)
range uint7 Percent of the travel each side of the centre, 1–100. 0: the motion's default, twitch 60, scan 70, wag 50, nod 40, circle 50
count uint5 Wag, nod: cycles; circle: turns. 1–20. 0: wag 3, nod 2, circle 1
x int16 Move: −100 to 100, percent of the way from the centre to an endpoint, + right. Nudge: −200 to 200, relative. Needed by move and nudge
y int16 As x; + is up
period_ms uint16 A cycle of wag, nod, a turn of circle, a sweep of scan there and back: 200–60000. 0: wag 700, nod 800, circle 2400, scan 6000
interval_min_ms, interval_max_ms uint20 Twitch: the pause between glances, chosen between the two: each 100–600000, min no more than max. Both 0: 1000 to 5000. Both or neither
time_limit_ms uint22 Twitch, scan: stop, and hold, after this long: 100–3600000. 0: no limit. A timed scan doesn't stop yet, over Cyphal, the console or the HTTP API alike: a fault in the shared holo engine, which looks at the time only between a motion's segments, and a scan has no gap between them
duration_ms uint16[<=1] Center, move, nudge: how long to take, 0–10000; 0 is at once. Empty: by the distance, 250 ms and 500 ms more for each half of the travel

A field the motion doesn't use is ignored, whatever it holds. The odd widths are the ranges', to keep the request to three frames.

The board checks and starts the motion with the code POST /api/v1/holo/motion runs once it has read its JSON, as the host link's requests do: the fields the motion uses, the times in seconds as the route's interval_s and time_s are, and nothing else. So a motion is checked and refused as one over HTTP is, in the same words, which name the JSON API's fields:

result.code When
bad request A field out of range: `range` is 1..100, `x` and `y` for move are -100..100, `interval_s` is [min, max], each 0.1..600, min first (one of the twitch's interval fields alone, or min over max), `time_s` is 0.1..3600. A kind that is none: `motion` is center, move, nudge, twitch, wag, nod, scan, circle, (the JSON API's words, cut, as every message is, to 64 bytes)
not ready The holo can't move: result.reason says why (not fitted, uncalibrated) and which axis, and result.message says it in words: holo: refused, uncalibrated (tilt)
busy The board is still working through four earlier requests: send it again

Nothing moves when a motion is refused. stop and off are never refused for the holo's state. This board moves an axis without saved endpoints over its default travel (the holo), so it never refuses one as uncalibrated: only an axis whose servo didn't attach is refused, as not fitted.

Light¶

r2.holo.Light.0.1, on light: the holo's light, with the modes a dome panel's own holos have. On this board the light is the LED ring: on is the ring's solid, the others are the ring's modes of the same names, and Status's ring shows the result.

Field Type What it is
mode uint8 0 off, 1 on, 2 pulse, 3 flicker, 4 blink, 5 leia
period_ms uint16 Pulse, blink: 200–60000. 0: pulse 2000, blink 1000
time_limit_ms uint22 100–3600000: a timed mode, after which the ring goes back to what it was doing (mode, colour and level; Light never changes the ring's brightness). 0: for good; for leia, 30 s
level uint8[<=1] Percent, 0–100: the share of the ring's brightness this mode lights at (0 lights nothing). Empty: 100. Ignored for off and leia
colour Colour[<=1] The ring's colour for this mode. Empty: the colour it has now (white if it has never had one)

The level is a share of the ring's brightness (leds bright, the board's settings), not a brightness of its own: so the light at full is the ring at the brightness the user set, which keeps a ring powered from the board within its limit (the LED page) whatever a controller asks for. Status's ring shows the product, the brightness the ring lights at.

The board runs the code PATCH /api/v1/leds runs, with the same checks and words: a mode with its colour if given, the level (100 if not given), a pulse's or a blink's period, and time_s for a time limit (the LED page has the same from the console).

result.code When
bad request `level` is 0..100, `period_ms` is 200..60000, `time_s` is 0.1..3600 (a time limit under 100 ms or over an hour), or a mode that is none: `mode` is off, on, pulse, flicker, blink or leia
busy The board is still working through four earlier requests

Leia is the message from Leia: the light flickers at full for the time limit and then goes back to what it was doing, and the holo turns to the centre if it can. The light half always runs. The response's centred (bool) says whether the centre did too; when it didn't, result.code is still 0 and result.reason and result.message give the centre's refusal. The flicker is at level 100 in the colour given, or the ring's own; the centre is the motion route's center, at its default pace, so Status shows center as the last motion asked for. A time limit out of range refuses the whole request, before either half runs.

Another light or ring command during a timed mode replaces it, and the wait ends.

Scene¶

r2.holo.Scene.0.1, on scene: apply a saved scene, or end the one running to its end, as POST /api/v1/scenes/apply and /scenes/end and the host link's SCENE and END do.

Field Type What it is
action uint8 0 apply, 1 end
slot uint8 Apply: the scene's slot, 1–255; 0 to apply it by name
name uint8[<=32] Apply with slot 0: the scene's name

The board loads the scene saved with that slot or name and applies it with the code POST /api/v1/scenes/apply runs; end is POST /api/v1/scenes/end's. The refusals carry the HTTP API's words:

result.code When
bad request action is none (`action` is 0 (apply) or 1 (end)); apply with slot 0 and no name (give `slot` (1..255), or `name` with slot 0); a name that can't be a scene's (give `name`: 1-32 letters, digits, spaces, _ . and -)
not found No scene with that slot (no scene with slot 7) or name (no scene 'cantina'); for end, no scene running to its end (no scene is running to its end)
not playable Its screen part can't be shown, its file missing included (screen: no such file: /clips/a.mov)
not ready Its holo motion can't run

A part that fails stops the parts after it: the screen, then the LEDs, then the holo. A scene applied starts and ends as one applied any other way: Event carries its scene_started, and its scene_ended with why it ended; Status's scene shows it while it runs to its end.

Screen¶

r2.holo.Screen.0.1, on screen: what the screen shows, and its backlight, as POST /api/v1/screen/show, DELETE /api/v1/screen and PATCH /api/v1/screen do.

Field Type What it is
show uint8 0 leave what shows, 1 a file, 2 a colour, 3 the calibration crosshair, 4 nothing (a clip stops; the panel sleeps with its backlight off)
loop bool A file that is a clip or an animation: play it forever. Not with loops
loops uint16 A clip or an animation: play it this many times, 1–1000. 0: a clip once, an animated GIF as the file says
whole_frame bool A file: decode each frame whole, rather than streamed in blocks
colour Colour A colour: which
backlight uint8[<=1] Percent, 0–100, until the board next starts. Empty: as it is. With show 0, this is all the request does
path uavcan.file.Path.2.0 A file: a QuickTime Motion-JPEG clip (.mov), a PNG, a baseline JPEG or a GIF

The board runs the code POST /api/v1/screen/show runs for a file, a colour or the crosshair, DELETE /api/v1/screen's for nothing, and then, if backlight is given, PATCH /api/v1/screen's. The file is checked before the response, so a refusal means the screen didn't change, and the backlight is set only once what shows has been done. show 0 with no backlight changes nothing, and is answered ok. A path is taken as the HTTP API takes it: from the root of the volume, at most 159 bytes (of the 255 the type has room for), or with the volume's own root in front (/data/clips/intro.mov).

result.code When
bad request A path the board refuses ('..' is not allowed in a path: /../a.png, a path may be at most 159 bytes, a path starts with /, …, a control character or a NUL in it), loop with loops (give `loop` (forever) or `loops` (a number of times), not both), loops over 1000, backlight over 100 (`backlight` is 0..100), a show that is none
not found No such file (no such file: /clips/a.mov)
not playable A file it can't show: the codec, the size, not a clip or an image. The message says which, as the HTTP API's does (/big.png: 400x300 is larger than the 240x240 screen)

Ring¶

r2.holo.Ring.0.1, on ring: the LED ring, as PATCH /api/v1/leds and the host link's LEDS do. Send only what changes: a new mode starts it from its beginning, a new colour or loop alone restarts the mode running with it, and the brightness applies at once, until the board next starts. A request with nothing in it changes nothing.

Field Type What it is
mode uint8[<=1] 0 off, 1 solid, 2 wipe, 3 rainbow, 4 flicker, 5 pulse, 6 blink. Empty: the mode running
colour Colour[<=1] For every mode but off and rainbow. Empty: the colour it has (white if it has never had one)
loop bool[<=1] Wipe, rainbow: again and again, or once. Empty: once with a new mode; without one, as it is
brightness uint8 Percent, 1–100. 0: as it is
period_ms uint16 Pulse, blink: 200–60000. 0: pulse 2000, blink 1000

The board runs the code PATCH /api/v1/leds runs, with what the request gives and nothing else, as the host link's LEDS does. brightness is the ring's own brightness (leds bright, GET /api/v1/leds's brightness), the one Light's level is a share of: Ring never sets a level. A new mode starts at level 100, so it undoes a Light's level; a colour, a loop or a period alone keeps the level the ring has, as PATCH /api/v1/leds does. Status's ring shows the product.

result.code When
bad request `mode` is off, solid, wipe, rainbow, flicker, pulse or blink (a mode over 6), `brightness` is 1..100, `period_ms` is 200..60000

ListScenes¶

r2.holo.ListScenes.0.1, on list_scenes: the saved scenes, one a call. Ask for index 0, 1, 2… until the name comes back empty, as uavcan.register.List lists registers. The order is the order they were first saved in (GET /api/v1/scenes).

Field Type What it is
index uint8 The request: 0-based
count uint8 Scenes saved, 0–16. A save or a delete between calls shifts the list, and this shows it
slot uint8 The scene's slot; 0 when it has none
name uint8[<=32] Its name; empty once index reaches count

The board counts its saved scenes by their stored lengths alone, in GET /api/v1/scenes' order, and reads and parses only the one asked for, so a call costs about the same whatever its index. result is ok, or busy with the board's queue full, or with the scenes not readable at that moment (busy: the scenes can't be read now); count is then 0. A saved scene that doesn't parse, which only a restored backup could bring (the board checks a scene before saving it), is counted, and answered busy at its index; GET /api/v1/scenes leaves it out instead.

MediaInfo¶

r2.holo.MediaInfo.0.1, on media_info: a clip's or an image's details from its header, without showing it, as GET /api/v1/media/info gives them. Optional: for a controller that needs more than the names and sizes uavcan.file.List and uavcan.file.GetInfo give.

Field Type What it is
path uavcan.file.Path.2.0 The request
kind uint8 0 not a clip or an image, 1 clip, 2 image, 3 animation
format uint8 0 none, 1 mov, 2 PNG, 3 JPEG, 4 GIF
width, height uint16 Pixels
frames uint16 A clip or an animation: in one pass; 1 for an image
fps float16 A clip or an animation; 0 for an image
duration_ms uint32 A clip or an animation: one pass; 0 for an image

result.code is 0 when the board can show it. A file it can't show is not playable, with why in the message, and still described when it is a kind the board knows. (The HTTP API answers that case with 200 and playable: false.) No such file is not found.

The board reads the file's header with the code GET /api/v1/media/info runs, with its checks and words:

result.code When
bad request No path (give `path`, from the root of the volume: /clips/intro.mov), or one the board refuses, in the HTTP API's words ('..' is not allowed in a path: /../a.png)
not found no such file or directory: /clips/a.mov
not playable <path>: <why>, the words of the HTTP API's why (/big400.png: 400x300 is larger than the 240x240 screen), cut to 64 bytes; a file that is no clip or image at all (/notes.txt: not a clip or an image (.mov, .png, .jpg, .gif)), and a directory, with kind 0 and every field 0
busy The second worker already holds two requests

An image is 1 frame, 0 fps and 0 ms, a GIF of one frame among them. Reading a header can take a while (a JPEG is read whole, and a GIF's every frame is looked at), so MediaInfo is carried out with Scene and Screen requests, by the second worker, never holding up the setpoints: on the bench a median of 16 ms over the 45 clips and images in the board's tree, 125 ms for an 837 KB GIF of 30 frames. What a header is read into is PSRAM first, so a MediaInfo takes little of the internal RAM a playing clip needs (4 to 9 KB, where it took up to 29 KB before 2026-11-21: the GIF decoder, the clip's tables, the JPEG's bytes and libpng's state; a JPEG's decoder stays in internal RAM).

The files¶

uavcan.file.List.0.2 (service 406) and uavcan.file.GetInfo.0.2 (405), the standard services, over the board's storage volume: what fs ls and the HTTP API's /api/v1/media see. Nothing on Cyphal writes, moves or deletes a file: there is no uavcan.file.Read, Write or Modify.

Paths are from the volume's root, /, as in the rest of this interface and in the HTTP API, with its rules (at most 159 bytes, no .., no empty part; the volume's own root in front is taken too). An empty path is the root as well, and a / at the end of a directory's path, as uavcan.file.Path's convention has it, is optional.

List gives a directory's entries one a call: entry_index 0, 1, 2… until entry_base_name comes back empty. They are in byte order of their names, directories among them, each with a / after its name, so a client tells them apart without a GetInfo: the order the storage volume (littlefs) keeps a directory in, and so the order fs ls prints and GET /api/v1/media walks. A directory that doesn't exist, a path that is a file or that the board refuses, and an index past the last all give the empty name: the type has no other answer. A call reads the directory's names up to its index, about 0.1 ms an entry on the board: on the bench, the root's 44 entries (43 files and a directory) took 3.8 ms a call at index 0 and 8.4 ms at index 43, from a computer, against 3.5 ms for a uavcan.node.GetInfo. An entry added or removed between two calls shifts the ones after it, as the type warns.

GetInfo gives an entry's details:

Field On this board
error OK; NOT_FOUND (2) for no such file or directory; INVALID_VALUE (22) for a path the board refuses; IO_ERROR (5) for anything else, and when the board's queue of requests is full (try again)
size Bytes; 0 for a directory
unix_timestamp_of_last_modification When the file was last written, if the board's clock had been set by then (from the network); 0 otherwise
is_file_not_directory As it says
is_link Always false
is_readable True
is_writeable False: nothing on Cyphal writes a file

Both are carried out by the second worker, with Scene, Screen and MediaInfo requests, since either waits for the storage volume, which an upload over Wi-Fi holds through each write (on the bench, during a 1 MB upload, a List of the root's last entry took 82 ms in the median and up to half a second); so one may wait behind a MediaInfo or a still being shown. A GetInfo takes about 1.5 ms on the board at rest. With the board's queue full, GetInfo answers IO_ERROR, and List isn't answered at all: its empty name would read as the end of the list, and it carries no count, so the client times out and asks again.

Registers¶

Read and written with uavcan.register.Access and listed with uavcan.register.List, by any Cyphal tool. The console's cyphal register reads and writes the same table, with the same checks. Every mutable register is saved on the board, in NVS, as soon as it is written, and is part of a backup; none holds a credential. List gives them in the order of the tables below: the node's three, then each port's .id and .type, then the holo's seven.

A write the board can't take leaves the register as it was, and the response, which always carries the register's value after the write, shows it: a value out of range or not a name, one of another type, a port number another port has (below), or a .type register. A number of another type is taken when it converts exactly (a natural8, an integer64 or a real32 52 all write 52 to uavcan.node.id), and the name takes unstructured bytes as well as a string. A register that doesn't exist reads empty, and a write to one does nothing. The board's log says what was written and by which node, or why it wasn't. While the board is still writing earlier requests (four at once), another write is answered with the register as it is, unwritten: send it again.

The node:

Register Type Values Default Flags Takes effect
uavcan.node.id natural16[1] 0–127, or 65535 for plug-and-play. Anything else is refused 65535 mutable, persistent When the node next starts
r2.node.name string The name GetInfo reports: 1–50 characters, as cyphal name takes it r2.dome.holo.front mutable, persistent When the node next starts
uavcan.can.bitrate natural32[2] The first value is the bitrate: 125000, 250000, 500000 or 1000000. The second (CAN FD's data phase) is ignored, and reads back as the first [1000000, 1000000] mutable, persistent When the node next starts

These are the settings cyphal id, cyphal name and cyphal bitrate change: either way changes both. The name isn't uavcan.node.description: that standard register is a free-text note that doesn't change what GetInfo reports, and this one does.

The ports, one pair a port name (status, event, axes, setpoint, motion, light, scene, screen, ring, list_scenes, media_info):

Register Type Values Default Flags Takes effect
uavcan.pub.<name>.id, uavcan.sub.<name>.id, uavcan.srv.<name>.id natural16[1] A subject-ID 0–8191 or a service-ID 0–511; 65535 turns the port off The default mutable, persistent When the node next starts
uavcan.pub.<name>.type, and so on string The type, r2.holo.Status.0.1 — immutable, persistent —

So uavcan.pub.status.id is 2300 until written, and uavcan.srv.motion.type reads r2.holo.Motion.0.1. The .type registers let a tool such as Yakut call a service by its port name. The standard suggests 65535 as every port's default; the board starts with working defaults instead, so it can be used without configuring anything. The special registers for a register's minimum, maximum and default (<name><, <name>>, <name>=) aren't provided.

A number belongs to one port. Subject-IDs are one set, published and subscribed alike (a subscription on a subject the board publishes would take other nodes' messages of that type for its own), and service-IDs another. Within each, a number is one of the node's own fixed ports' (subjects 7509, 7510 and 8166, services 384, 385, 405, 406, 430 and 435) or one r2.holo port's, never two: a write of a number another port has is refused with the port that has it ("subject 2301 is uavcan.pub.event.id's"), and the register keeps its value. A subject and a service may share a number, and any number of ports may be off (65535). So two ports swap through a free number, or through off. A saved number that breaks the rule anyway, which only a configuration from elsewhere can bring (a backup from another firmware), turns the later port in the table off as the board starts, with a warning in its log; its register then reads 65535.

The holo:

Register Type Values Default Flags Takes effect
r2.holo.horizontal.endpoints_us, r2.holo.vertical.endpoints_us natural16[2] Closed, then open, in µs, each within the servo's absolute range (1000–2000 on this board) and not equal; closed above open turns the axis round. [0, 0] goes back to the firmware's default range, and clears the drive policy with it [1150, 1900], [1300, 1950]: the default range, not saved mutable, persistent At once: the next motion uses them
r2.holo.horizontal.release, r2.holo.vertical.release bit[3] The drive policy, for the closed end, the open end and between (the ends are 2 % of the travel wide): true lets the servo go once it has settled, false keeps driving it [false, false, false] mutable, persistent At the servo's next frame
r2.holo.horizontal.settle_ms, r2.holo.vertical.settle_ms natural16[1] How long a command is driven before a release takes effect: 0–65535, under 100 raised to 100 100 mutable, persistent At once
r2.touch.reporting bit[1] Touch events on or off false (true in a build with BOARD_TOUCH_AUTOSTART) mutable, persistent At once, and at every start

The endpoints are the ones holo endpoints sets, and the drive policy the one the web app and PUT /api/v1/servos/policy set: the same saved values, the servos' own, so a change either way shows the other way. Two rules come with them from the servos: clearing the endpoints ([0, 0], as holo endpoints <axis> clear and DELETE /api/v1/servos/calibration do) clears the drive policy too, back to holding everywhere and 100 ms; and a drive policy once saved has a settle time of 100 ms at least, so a release written over a policy never saved also saves 100 ms. Status's horizontal and vertical say whether each axis is calibrated and turned round. An axis whose servo isn't fitted reads [0, 0], [false, false, false] and 0, and refuses a write (the axis has no servo (not fitted)). A refused write keeps the register as it was, with the reason on the console (closed and open are each 1000 to 2000 us, and not the same; [0, 0] goes back to the default range, a settle time is 0 to 65535 ms).

A read of these six over Cyphal is carried out by the node's worker, as a write is, from the servos themselves (reading them can wait on a save in progress, which the node's own task mustn't): a change made on the console or over HTTP reads over Cyphal at once. With the board's queue of requests full, such a read isn't answered (uavcan.register.Access has no way to say busy), and the client asks again.

r2.touch.reporting is one of the board's settings: the same one as touch.reporting in /api/v1/settings and the console's settings touch. A write turns touch reporting on or off at once and saves it, so that a controller sets it once, and the board starts with touch reporting on or off as it says; DELETE /api/v1/settings and a factory reset put it back to its default. touch on and touch off on the console change it for the moment without saving; the register reads what is in effect, so after a touch off it reads false until it is written or the board restarts. It isn't a credential or an identity, so it is in a backup (NVS holo_cfg, touch, with the board's other settings).

Commands¶

uavcan.node.ExecuteCommand.1.3, on service 435:

Command Number Does
Restart 65535 Restarts the board a second after the response (by when it has gone). The node comes back with the registers as they now are
Factory reset 65532 The node's registers back to their defaults (the node-ID, name and bitrate, every port), as cyphal reset does, and the board's settings to theirs (the boot scene, the backlight, the LEDs' brightness and count, touch reporting, which is the register r2.touch.reporting), as DELETE /api/v1/settings does; then a restart, a second after the response. Kept: the scenes, the files, the servos' calibration and drive policy (their registers keep their values), the network and the web app's settings, and the host link's mode and pins, so the node is still on the bus afterwards, asking for a node-ID by plug-and-play
Identify 65529 The ring blinks white, once a second, and the screen shows the crosshair for 5 seconds, then both go back to what they were doing
Store persistent states 65530 Nothing to do: every register is saved when it is written. Answers success
Anything else Refused: bad command. Power off, begin software update and emergency stop included

A response's status is success when the command was taken, before a restart; failure when the board is still working through four earlier requests, or for identify, which is carried out with the Scene and Screen requests, two of those (send it again); internal error if a factory reset couldn't save, in which case nothing is reset (what was saved before is put back) and the board doesn't restart. output is always empty.

Identify is a five-second scene, identify, applied as a scene of the board's own would be: the web app, the HTTP API's GET /api/v1/scenes and the events show it running, and it ends a scene that is running to its end. At its end the screen, the ring and the holo go back to what they were doing before it, as with any scene whose then is restore (a twitch or a scan starts again; a clip on its way to its end doesn't), or, if it ended a scene that restores, to what that scene would have restored.

Sizes and bus load¶

Each type's size, from its definition ("least" with every variable-length field empty). A frame is about 130 bits on the wire, so 130 µs at 1 Mbit/s.

Type Least: bytes, frames Worst case: bytes, frames
Status 86, 13 375, 54
Event 5, 1 265, 39
Axes 4, 1 4, 1
Motion request 17, 3 19, 3
Light request 8, 2 12, 2
Scene request 3, 1 35, 6
Screen request 9, 2 265, 39
Ring request 6, 1 11, 2
ListScenes request, response 1, 1; 6, 1 1, 1; 102, 15
MediaInfo request, response 1, 1; 17, 3 256, 37; 81, 12
uavcan.file.List request, response 9, 2; 5, 1 264, 38; 260, 38
uavcan.file.GetInfo request, response 1, 1; 13, 3 256, 37; 13, 3
Any other response 3, 1 67, 10 (a refusal with a 64-byte message)

The worst cases with a path count 255 bytes; this board's paths are at most 159. In practice a Status with a 20-byte path and a 10-byte scene name is 116 bytes, 17 frames; an event with a 20-byte path, 4 or 5.

What the board sends, at 1 Mbit/s:

Traffic Frames a second Share of the bus
Heartbeat, once a second 1 0.013 %
Port list, 24 frames every 10 s 2.4 0.03 %
Status once a second, 17 frames (13 with nothing showing and no scene) 17 0.22 %
Status four times a second (while things change) 68 0.88 %
Axes while moving, ten a second 10 0.13 %
Axes at rest 1 0.013 %
All of it, while moving and changing about 81 about 1.1 %

A controller streaming setpoints at 50 a second adds 50 frames, 0.65 %. The board hands its frames to the CAN controller one at a time, highest priority first, so a setpoint's echo in axes or a service's response waits behind at most one frame of a long Status, not the whole of it.

Not on Cyphal¶

These stay on Wi-Fi, the USB console, or the host link:

  • Uploading clips and images, and deleting or moving files.
  • The network, the web app's settings, and any password or token.
  • The configuration backup (nvs_export, nvs_import).
  • Firmware updates: over Wi-Fi, as now.
  • Saving, changing and deleting scenes, and applying a scene given whole: the web app and the HTTP API do those. Cyphal applies a saved one.
  • The board's settings (the boot scene and the levels it starts with) other than by a factory reset, and the host link's own settings.
  • Raw servo moves, for finding endpoints: the web app's calibration, servo_move and holo endpoints do that. The endpoints themselves are registers.
  • Catching up on missed events: the HTTP API's and the host link's "events after N" has no Cyphal counterpart; Status is the catch-up.
  • Diagnostics on the bus (uavcan.diagnostic.Record), time synchronisation, the IMU and the battery.
  • The console's holo led and holo leia, which still do nothing on this board: the Light service is what drives the ring as the holo's light.
